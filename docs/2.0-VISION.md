# Hood Vs Hood 2.0 — Vision & Scope Notes

Not a build plan. This is a capture of a design conversation about where the
game could go *after* a token launch, once there's an actual community to
design for instead of guessing. Explicitly deferred - revisit if/when the
project has real traction. Nothing here should be started without a fresh
scoping pass at that time.

## The core constraint

An NFT that's been played constantly shouldn't keep contributing at full
weight forever - same problem as an over-bred CryptoKitty. Buying a fighter
that's been fought a thousand times shouldn't move the needle the way a
fresh one does.

**Resolution (the simple version, not the sybil-detection version):** decay
a fighter's contribution based on its own total lifetime matches played -
something like `weight = base / sqrt(totalMatches)`, computed entirely
on-chain, per-NFT. No cross-wallet collusion analysis needed; the earlier
"prevent two wallets farming each other" framing turned out to be solving
the wrong problem. Once a fighter's weight decays past a threshold, it's
mechanically "retired" - no separate flag required, just a consequence of
the curve.

## Soulbound achievement records

- **Standard:** ERC-721 + [EIP-5192](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-5192)
  (minimal soulbound extension - adds `locked()`, blocks transfer). Not
  ERC-1155 (each record is inherently one-of-one, not stackable), and not
  EIP-2535 "Diamond" (that's a contract-upgradeability pattern, unrelated
  to token semantics - a dead end we ruled out).
- **Minted to:** the fighter NFT's own [ERC-6551](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-6551)
  token-bound account, not the human's main wallet - ties the achievement
  to *that specific Hoodie*, not to a person who could otherwise farm
  across every NFT they own.
- **Per-token metadata:** opponent wallet, opponent NFT contract + token ID,
  timestamp. Each record names a specific match.
- **Anti-farming:** canonical hash of `(walletA, nftA, walletB, nftB)`
  (sorted so order doesn't matter) checked against a `usedPairings` mapping
  before minting. Only blocks the exact same pair farming each other twice -
  sufficient once the decay curve above is doing the real work.
- **"Level" is a derived view, not a second token.** `level(address)` sums/
  counts the soulbound records a wallet or TBA holds (optionally weighted
  by the decay curve). Gets the "simple incrementing balance" UX for free
  without building a parallel ERC-20.

## Retired fighters

Once decay drops a fighter below the "active" threshold, it retires. Open
questions on what that actually looks like, in the order they came up:

- **Cosmetics as a visible record.** Players slowly earn/collect clothing
  and cosmetic items while actively playing - a store (possibly run through
  OnChainHoodies infrastructure) where cosmetics are bought/earned during
  active play. On retirement, the fighter's current look **locks soulbound**
  permanently - the cosmetic set becomes a frozen visual record of their
  play history. No separate "trophy" UI needed; the look *is* the trophy.
- **Background cameo.** Retired fighters appear as animated NPCs in arena
  backgrounds, Street-Fighter-crowd style - silently "talking" via chat
  bubbles above their heads, plus a cheering animation (would need to be
  generated). A real-world echo of retired athletes doing commentary
  instead of playing.
- **Passive fee share.** Retired fighters could collect a cut of match fees
  generated in arenas they appear in - a "legacy" reward for veteran
  fighters.

  **The exploit risk, self-identified during the conversation and worth
  repeating here:** if retiring unlocks passive income, there's pressure to
  retire early/cheap to farm it, which inverts the entire point. Guardrail:
  gate retirement behind a genuinely high match-count threshold (a real
  achievement, not a shortcut), and keep passive fee-share modest relative
  to what an active fighter's owner can earn. Retirement needs to stay
  *prestige*, not the economically optimal move - otherwise it hollows out
  the active player base chasing background-NPC royalties instead of
  playing.

## Arenas as ownable NFTs

The natural endgame of the above: players eventually buy/build out their
own arena - choice of NFT arena backdrop, which retired fighters appear in
it, background elements (buildings, cars, etc.), cosmetics on display as
achievements. Fee-splitting between an arena's owner and the retired
fighters featured in it could get genuinely interesting (and genuinely
complex) - a lot of interlocking systems (ownership, fee splits, cosmetic
scarcity) that need a real economy to design against, not guesswork.

## Marketplace

OpenSea won't surface token-bound-account contents (tokens, LP positions,
soulbound achievements held inside an NFT's own TBA) as marketplace
metadata for one specific collection - that's not coming from them.
Realistic path is a custom marketplace for this collection that *can*
surface it: TBA holdings, soulbound achievement history, retired status,
arena ownership. Natural fit with `hoodiearcade.com` as the hub domain
already in hand.

**Licensing doesn't have to match the game's.** The game itself is CC0 -
open by design, no other way to earn trust with a wallet-connect game (see
README). The marketplace is a separate product and doesn't have to be open
source just because the game is - it's a legitimate path to actually get
paid to keep running this. If the NFTs and/or token do well enough to pay
for itself independent of marketplace fees, open-sourcing the marketplace
too becomes a real option rather than a giveaway. Keep this decision open
rather than committing either way now - it's cheap to stay flexible and
expensive to reverse a public open-source commitment later.

## Sequencing

All of the above is **2.0, post-token-launch, contingent on the game
actually having a community to build for.** Nothing here is scoped for
implementation yet. If/when it's time: this doc is the memory, not the
plan - do a fresh scoping pass against however the project and community
actually look at that point, not against these notes verbatim.
